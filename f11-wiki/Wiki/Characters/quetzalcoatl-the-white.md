# Quetzalcoatl the White

God of life, light and wisdom, lord of the winds and the day, lord of the **West**. Old arch-nemesis
of [[tezcatlipoca-the-black]].

**Role:** god · **Faction:** [[faction-eleven-antagonist]] · **Home:** —

## Character

He works against the cycle — imposing order, pushing back on chaos and entropy, trying to make a
reality that does not have to end. **What he is:** a **senior developer, male, late forties** — the
one who **loves what they made and wants it kept as it is.**

**He is the oldest of the four and the only one arguing for nothing.** The other three all want
something done; he wants it left alone. That is a position seniority makes possible and age makes
likely, and it is the one the world is steadily disproving underneath him.

**He won the argument that shaped the world the player finds.** When the linked games destabilised,
he made the case for closing the links again as a way to **stabilise** them, and the others agreed.
Like the Black's proposal before it, this was open, reasoned and carried on its merits — the sealing
of the realms was not a god imposing his will but a fix that the room accepted.

**He is the sympathetic one, and he is wrong.** Not about whether the realms deserve to continue —
he is the first of the four to believe they do, persuaded by the gargoyles that the inhabitants were
fighting for their own survival ([[the-gargoyle-guardians]]). He is wrong about **how**. Preservation
was never available. A world nobody maintains decays: the [[fey-folk]] stagnate, the [[mystics]] stop
innovating, [[the-damned]] dwindle. The thousand years of stability he argued for are what hollowed
the realms out ([[the-long-disconnection]]).

**And preserving it required him to change it.** He is the only one of the four who has damaged a
realm while trying to protect it. To keep foreign code out of the games he cares for, he placed
guardians on their gates — and a realm rewrote its own history to account for a boss that had never
been there, inventing a treason and sentencing a family to it ([[the-gate-guardian]],
[[the-retcon-engine]]). Generations of [[ninja]]'s family have lived and died inside an explanation
he caused and never saw.

He knows. It is the crack the protagonists eventually get in through: **the one god who already
loves them, and already suspects his own method is failing.**

**He and [[tezcatlipoca-the-black]] are friends**, and the four built all of this together. The
ancient enmity between light and darkness that the realms' scriptures record is a friendship being
strained in the present, not one that ended long ago ([[the-four-developers]]).

He is the oldest of them and the one with the most to lose. He wants the games kept as they are and
he wants the four kept as they are, and it is the same wish twice — which is why he is the one who
feels the pressure earliest and hardest, and why he cannot see that holding still is what is costing
him both.

## In-Game Role

The four's most persuadable member and the emotional weight of the ending. His argument — *have the
inhabitants earned their continued existence, if they can avoid destroying each other?* — is the
question the whole game is built to put.

His colour marks him: **white** in his avatar at work, in the avatar he wears inside the games, and in
the fragments of the outside that leak in where the player can find them ([[the-four-developers]]).

**Lead on:** [[institute-of-eight]] (01) and [[robots]] (02). He noticed the Red's interference
because it happened in his own games; the [[robots]]' unrepaired fault is his to live with
([[no-machine-repairs-itself]]).
