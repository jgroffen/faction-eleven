# The Eleventh Faction — The Ancients

The eleventh faction **is** the Ancients, and the Ancients are **humans**. They built the gates
between the realms, then locked and guarded them, and they **extended the natural cycle** of
disconnection — for reasons nobody now knows.

**All awareness that an eleventh faction ever existed has been lost.** Rediscovering that they
existed, what they did, and why, is the mystery the whole game is built to solve.

**Homeland:** not established — whether the Ancients hold an eleventh realm of their own is
unrecorded.

## Identity

Human, in a roster of robots, fey, werebeasts, aliens and the damned. The player spends ten
stories as everything except a human, and the thing behind it all is us.

They are defined entirely by what they made and what they did with it:

- They **built the gates** that join near-by realms ([[sealed-interplane-gates]]).
- They **numbered the realms** — the catalogue that arranges the ten in a ring is theirs, and
  it is why the title of the game is a number ([[the-wheel-of-realms]]).
- They **locked and guarded** those gates.
- They **extended the cycle** of disconnection past its natural length, until the other factions
  became myths to each other ([[the-long-disconnection]]).
- They left behind a braille-like script ([[the-ancient-language]]).

Their motive is deliberately unwritten — "unknown reasons" is the in-fiction answer, and the thing
the player is chasing.

## They Are Acting Now

Everything else they have done is prehistory. This is not:

> **An Ancient is manipulating [[the-guardians-of-night]]** — a secret cross-realm group — **to
> start trouble with other factions.**

The Guardians in turn incited three realms into war ([[the-broken-barrier]]). So the game's live
conflict traces back to a single Ancient, through people who have never heard of the Ancients.

Two things this changes:

- **The antagonist now has a reachable proxy.** [[chapter-02]] can deliver a real villain and a real
  revelation without spending any of the eleventh faction's mystery — a three-deep chain, *an
  Ancient → the Guardians of Night → three realms at war*, where the player only ever sees the
  bottom two rungs.
- **Their method is consistent.** They did not conquer the multiverse; they got the realms to seal
  themselves in ([[the-realm-barriers]]) and are now getting them to fight each other. An antagonist
  who works exclusively through other people's convictions is one who can stay invisible
  indefinitely — which is exactly the state the game starts in.

**Note the wording: "an" Ancient — singular.** Whether that means one individual acting alone, or is
simply how the manipulation reaches the Guardians, is **not established**. It is the only hint of an
*individual* rather than a faction.

Their motive remains the largest open question in the design ([[story-outline-requirement]]).
Knowing they are active does not say what for.

## Standing

Not a faction the player fights so much as one they **uncover**. Because the ten factions have
forgotten the Ancients entirely, the eleventh faction has no diplomatic standing at all: no ally,
no enemy, no one who remembers.

The one piece of moral ambiguity already in the material is worth protecting: the Institute's gate
"was sealed and guarded **for a reason**", and when [[ninja]] finally breaks it open, green-skins
pour through. The player's first act against the Ancients' work is arguably a mistake.

## Notes

Design consequence: because the eleventh faction is revealed across all ten stories, its reveal
schedule is coupled to the player's story order and to [[chapter-based-release]]. Whatever
ordering the game settles on has to keep the mystery legible from any path the player takes.

The three questions the game must eventually answer — **that they existed, what they did, why** —
are a natural three-act spine for the reveal, and they map onto a chaptered release.
