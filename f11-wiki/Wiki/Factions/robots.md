# Robots

A civilisation of old, defunct machines who **do not know how to fix themselves**. The autofix bots —
the only models able to repair damaged robots and build new ones from spare parts — all stopped
working, and the realm's history says the Creators destroyed them. It gives no reason. The Creators
are long gone; the robots have fallen into disrepair and despair.

**Homeland:** [[realm-02]] — see [[the-wheel-of-realms]]

## The Purge Never Happened

**The Creators destroyed nothing.** The account [[realm-02]] carries is a retcon, generated to cover
something the realm could not otherwise explain: every fixbot in the world failing at roughly the same
time ([[the-retcon-engine]]).

What actually happened is a property of what a fixbot is. Their repair skill edits code, so they are
**code-aware underneath** ([[a-heal-is-a-patch]]) — and code-awareness makes a fixbot progressively
unreliable once it has been exposed to another realm's [[the-setting-statement|Setting Statement]].
[[the-linking-of-the-realms]] exposed all of them. Most bugged out and crashed. Some froze.

**The generated history has no motive in it**, and that is the seam. A realm that can explain the
Creators' departure, their works and their relics in detail has, at the centre of its worst
catastrophe, an act with no stated reason — and nobody has ever found that strange.

## Identity

Fractured rather than unified. Some bots found a new purpose after the Creators left; others lack
the neural network depth to develop one and have shut down or gone insane. The ones with purpose
have developed **varied goals, some constructive and some destructive** — so the faction is at war
with itself as much as anything else.

Their protagonist is [[autofix-alto]], a late-model Autofix and the only known functioning example.

**Currency:** in play, a refining chain of **scrap → construction materials → modules and
components** ([[autofix-skill-tree]]) · **Role archetype:** Medic

**Game style:** [[robots-incremental-exploration]] — an exploration platformer with incremental
growth, replacing the prototype's Civ-style region control. The **fiction was kept and the
strategy layer replaced**: the vanished fixbots, the lone survivor and Autofix's awakening are
unchanged as beats.

## Standing

Not established beyond their own realm. The robots' story goal is internal — unite the robots by
controlling all regions — with the multiverse plot entering as **sealed interplane gates** they
discover and cannot open alone. That inability is the hook that forces contact with other
factions.

Notably, a **mini-fix bot** made by Autofix's line turns up inside the Institute of Eight's story
as an ancestor spirit housed in a toy car ([[institute-of-eight]]), which is the earliest
cross-faction thread in the material.

## Notes

The story is built on a question the faction cannot answer and the player eventually can: why every
fixbot stopped, and why the only account of it names a culprit and no reason. [[autofix-alto]] is the one
who did not crash — he froze instead, and something reached him ([[the-first-signal]]).

The robots **lead chapter one** ([[chapter-based-release]]), which makes Autofix's story the
carrier of the game's first contact with [[faction-eleven-antagonist]]'s work: sealed gates the
robots cannot open alone.

Currency and death mechanic are **lore-only** — see [[faction-currencies]] and
[[death-and-return]].
