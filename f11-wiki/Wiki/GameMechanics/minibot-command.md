# Minibot Command

[[autofix]] builds **minibots** from modules and components, and **controls** them — issuing
commands — within a **short range of himself**. Range and capability both come from
[[autofix-skill-tree]].

**State:** concept · **Category:** progression

**Implemented by:** _(no feature notes yet)_

## How It Works

- Minibots are **built**, not found, once the Robotics branch is unlocked.
- **All minibots only work within a short range of the Autofix bot.** Extending that range is the
  comms ladder, and it is the faction's core progression.
- **Max controllable minibots** is itself an upgrade, on the Upgrades branch.
- Minibots **can be upgraded** individually — and **upgrading consumes a minibot**. Specialising one
  costs you a unit of the workforce.

### Stored labour that spends like currency

Minibots are **both a workforce and a resource**. They work for you, and they are also what you
spend to get better ones: making a specialist **consumes** a minibot, reducing the stored labour you
had.

So every specialisation is a real cost — fewer hands now, better hands later — and the player is
repeatedly choosing between a broad workforce and a capable one. It also means the general
scrap-collecting minibot is never obsolete: it is the raw material of every specialist.

### The kinds

| Minibot | Role |
|---------|------|
| **General** | the first kind — collects **basic scrap parts** |
| **Shield-bot** | ideas for specialised later unlocks; all three help **disable robots that have gone mad or aggressive**, so Autofix can repair them |
| **Suppressor-bot** | " |
| **EMP-bot** | " |

The specialists are a **non-lethal combat suite**. The Robots' answer to a hostile robot is not to
kill it but to shut it down and fix it — which is the faction's whole character expressed as a
mechanic. There is no reason to destroy a robot when robots are the thing you are short of.

### Mini-fix bots are not minibots

**Mini-fix bot production is the culmination** of the tree — the point at which [[realm-02]] can
**essentially start to self-repair**, because bots that repair bots no longer need Autofix present.

But they are categorically different from minibots:

> Autofix can **control** every minibot he builds. **Mini-fix bots he can communicate with but not
> control** — even the ones he built. They are far more autonomous, like other robots. As with most
> repaired robots, they are happy to work with him; they simply are not his to command.

**And they are not leashed.** Where every minibot must stay within range of Autofix, mini-fix bots
are exempt — which is the entire point of them. The endgame of the tree is building things that
**do not need you**, which is exact for a character whose purpose is to give his people a future
rather than to be their future.

That autonomy is what makes [[mifix]] possible: a mini-fix bot that has been living its own life in
another realm for generations is not a malfunction, it is what mini-fix bots are.

## Why It's Fun

Not yet defined. The intended shape is visible though: the player graduates from doing
everything themselves, to having help, to having a workforce, to having a *civilisation that no
longer needs them* — which is exactly Autofix's stated purpose.

## Tuning

No values yet. **Range** and **max controllable minibots** are the two knobs that define every
