# Realm Nearness And Traversal

The realms are separate spaces with **ways of moving between them**, and they have a concept of
**nearness**: *close* realms are easier to traverse between than distant ones. Nearness is not
fixed — the realms run through **cycles** in which traversal goes from easy to almost
impossible.

**State:** concept · **Category:** traversal

**Implemented by:** _(no feature notes yet)_

## How It Works

Established:

- Each faction has its own realm; there is no shared map ([[the-multiverse-of-realms]]).
- Traversal between realms is possible by some means.
- **Nearness** determines difficulty of traversal between any two realms.
- Nearness **cycles over time**, from easy to almost impossible.
- The game starts at the difficult end of that cycle ([[the-long-disconnection]]).

Not yet established, and each is load-bearing:

- Whether nearness is a **number the player can see** or a felt quality.
- Whether the cycle **advances during play** — a moving graph that opens and closes routes as the
  story proceeds — or is fixed scenery for the duration of the game.
- Whether nearness is **symmetric** (A near B implies B near A).
- How traversal actually happens: [[sealed-interplane-gates]] are one mechanism, the Damned's
  **void** may be another, and whether there are others is unrecorded.

## Why It's Fun

Not yet stated by the author. The obvious candidate — a route network that changes under the
player, so the multiverse feels tidal rather than static — is the interviewer's reading, not the
author's claim.

## Tuning

No values yet. If the cycle advances during play, its period is the single most important number
in the game's structure, because it gates when stories can touch.
