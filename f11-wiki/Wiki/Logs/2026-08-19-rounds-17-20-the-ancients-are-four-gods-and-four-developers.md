# 2026-08-19 — Rounds 17-20: the Ancients are four gods, and four developers

## Details

The eleventh faction is settled at three layers: the realms remember nothing; the discoverable in-game truth is a pantheon of four (the Tezcatlipocas); the real truth is four software developers at a struggling indie studio, with the ten realms as ten computer games. Two long-blocking gaps in story-outline-requirement are closed: the Ancients' motive (four people disagreeing about whether to preserve, grow, harden or scrap what they built) and the shape of the ending (the protagonists unite the factions and prove no god is wholly right or wrong; the plot is on rails). New notes: the-four-gods, the-four-developers, the-retcon-engine, the-linking-of-the-realms, the-void, continuity-glitches, and character notes for all four. faction-eleven-antagonist rewritten around the three-layer structure - its original claim that the Ancients are humans survives intact at the bottom layer. Answered standing open questions: who set the Gate Guardian (the White, as access control; the Institute's generational sentence is retconned lore), why the robot gate is guarded, whether 'an Ancient' is an individual (the Blue), whether Val leads the Guardians of Night (she founded it; the Blue's Secret Society System chose her, and she already carried the Red's power), and why realm-07's gargoyles were spared (the Damned fear the Fey Folk). The robots' inability to repair themselves is reclassified as an unrepaired fault from the linking, not a decline from the sealing. Chapter structure moves to 8-10 chapters with multiple faction arcs per chapter and earlier cross-realm visits.
