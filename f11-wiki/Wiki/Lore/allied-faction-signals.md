# Allied Faction Signals

Scattered through the realms are **old devices that call another faction**. They are not gates and
they do not move anyone: they send a **signal** to a faction that can hear it.

**They are not the Ancients' work.** They were **a means for allied factions to contact each other
for aid** — ordinary infrastructure between friends, from the era before the realms were sealed.
When the factions became sealed, **the signals could no longer cross between realms**, and the
devices went quiet and were forgotten.

So this is how the multiverse comes back together: not by opening the locked gates, and not with
anything the jailer left behind, but by **getting the old alliances' telephones working again**.

**Canon:** proposed · **Era:** predates [[the-long-disconnection]]

| [[the-clay-jar]], holding an alien egg | [[realm-01]], the same shrine | [[aliens]] |
| [[odie]] | [[templar-monastery]], [[realm-03]] | [[robots]] |
| [[the-wand]] | [[templar-monastery]], [[realm-03]] | [[mystics]] |

Two patterns are already visible and worth protecting:

1. **A device is a piece of the faction it calls.** An autofix bot calls the robots. A wand calls
   the mystics. An alien egg calls the aliens. The signal is not a message — it is a *relative*,
   left behind.
2. **They cluster.** [[realm-01]]'s shrine holds two; [[templar-monastery]] holds two. These are
   the embassies of the old alliances — the places where friends kept a way to call each other.

That they are found in **shrines and monasteries** is the quiet tragedy: objects left by allies
for practical use, kept for generations as holy relics by people who no longer knew what a friend
was for.

The [[institute-of-eight]]'s family have kept their shrine for generations without knowing what
either object was. That is the shape of the whole game: the means of reconnection have been sitting
in these realms all along, venerated and misunderstood.

## Why They Stopped, And How They Start Again

**"Ancient" here means old, not [[faction-eleven-antagonist]].** The devices belong
to the factions themselves. The Ancients did not build them and did not need to destroy them — 
**sealing the realms was enough**, because a signal that cannot cross between realms is a signal
that does not arrive.

Which means every device in the game is **already working and already unheard**. Nothing needs
repairing except the space between. [[mifix]] makes the point sharply: he has been
awake for generations in a realm where nothing could reach him.

**A signal needs an opening to leave a realm.** A fully sealed realm is mute as well as deaf —
[[realm-02]] is sealed on every side, so [[autofix]]'s Quantum Comms cannot get out at all until
[[the-cracked-gate]]. This is why the devices went quiet: not because they failed, but because the
walls closed.

**Signals route, they do not leap.** A signal travels realm to realm through whatever gates are
open or cracked along the way — the second signal reaches [[realm-01]] from [[realm-03]] by way of
[[realm-09]] ([[the-second-signal]]). So the network's reach is a function of the gates' *states*,
and a single crack in the right place can connect realms that are nowhere near each other.

That is what makes [[the-cracked-gate]] the hinge of chapter one: a fracture in a single sealed
gate is the first place a signal can get through, and it is caused by [[ninja]] **losing**.

## Ties To The Antagonist

The Ancients' role here is entirely negative, and stronger for it: they did not confiscate the
means of contact, they simply made contact impossible and let the objects rot into relics. The
factions kept their telephones for generations without knowing the lines were cut.

## Player-Facing

Central. Finding, identifying and activating these devices looks like a primary player activity
across the whole game, and the symbol on [[the-clay-jar]] shows the intended method: the player
reads the object and understands what it is for.
