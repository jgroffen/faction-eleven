# The Ancient Language

A **braille-like written language** with its own custom character set — raised dots rather than
drawn letters, left by [[faction-eleven-antagonist]] in every realm they touched.

**It is not a language.** It is machine-readable data, and when the player learns to decode it they
are reading the developers' own words ([[the-four-developers]]).

**Canon:** proposed · **Era:** predates the ten factions' knowledge of each other

## The Story

Dots, not letters — a script you could read without seeing it. The factions treat it as sacred
writing left by someone older than memory, and copy it faithfully onto shrines and relics without
knowing what any of it says.

**It appears in every realm** because it is the one thing all ten realms have in common: not a
culture, not a history, but the substrate they are all built on.

What it decodes to is dev-layer text. Commit messages. TODOs. Variable names. Commented-out code. An
asset path. The player can be reading it for hours — patiently, correctly, a symbol at a time —
before understanding what kind of thing they are looking at.

**Each of the four leaves a different tell in it**, which is how attribution becomes possible late in
the game ([[the-four-developers]]).

## Why It Carries The Reveal

It is the **one channel [[the-retcon-engine]] cannot rewrite.** A realm generates history to cover
what is done to it, and that history is persuasive and internally consistent. The dots are not part
of the realm's account of itself; they sit underneath it. So the game has exactly two kinds of
evidence — contradictions between realms ([[continuity-glitches]]), which the player must infer from,
and this, which they can actually *read*.

This is how the metafiction lands without a cutscene.

## Ties

- [[faction-eleven-antagonist]] — whose it is.
- [[the-four-developers]] — what it decodes to.
- [[the-retcon-engine]] · [[continuity-glitches]] — the other evidence channel.
- [[the-clay-jar]] — carries the first symbol the player reads, in chapter one.

## Player-Facing

**Player-facing, and a genuinely solvable cipher — not set dressing.** Building the custom character
set is intended to be fun to make, and a braille-like code is the classic shape of a puzzle solved
across a whole game: found in one realm and read in another.

It must appear from **chapter one**, before there is anything to decode it against, because early
chapters ship and become unrevisable ([[chapter-based-release]]). [[the-clay-jar]] already carries a
symbol that tells the player what the jar is, which is the intended method in miniature: the player
reads an object and understands what it is for.
