# The Continuity System

The White's first stabilisation layer on the Black's self-evolving engine: it tracks the world's evolution into key continuity events and rejects any that would break continuity.

**Canon:** proposed · **Era:** predates the game worlds

## The Story

[[tezcatlipoca-the-black]] built an open world, persistent system that **evolves its own gameplay**,
and could not make it stable — a world that rewrites itself will eventually rewrite itself into
nonsense ([[the-four-developers]]).

**The White's answer was not to stop the evolution. It was to give it a spine.** The Continuity
System watches the world evolve, records the turns that matter as **key continuity events**, and
**rejects anything that would break them.** The world stays free to change; it is not free to
contradict what it has already established.

That is what made the engine shippable, and it is why there are ten games rather than one
abandoned experiment.

## Two Systems, Not One

The White built **two** stabilisation layers on the same engine, at two different times, and they do
opposite jobs. Keeping them apart is the whole point of this note:

| | [[the-continuity-system]] | [[the-retcon-engine]] |
|---|---|---|
| Built | at the founding, before there were worlds | after [[the-linking-of-the-realms]] |
| To stabilise | the Black's self-evolving engine | ten realms that had been connected |
| Job | **prevention** — refuse the contradiction | **consolidation** — retroactively rewrite history to force ten continuities into one |
| Acts | forward, as the world evolves | backward, on history already written |
| Trigger | every event, before it lands | only when an error is **detected**, after the fact |
| Scope | the one world it polices | whatever it can see — never all ten histories |
| Fails at | contradictions that already happened | **not causing new ones** |

## Why It Stopped Being Enough

**The Continuity System did not fail. It was asked a question it is not shaped to answer.**

It works by **refusing continuity-breaking events as they occur**, and while the ten worlds were
disconnected that was completely sufficient. Each world had its own continuity; each was policed
independently; no error got in. On its own terms it was a total success, and it stayed one for as
long as the worlds stayed apart.

**[[the-linking-of-the-realms]] did not present it with an event to reject.** It presented it with
**ten already-established continuities that now had to be a single one** — and every one of them was
individually valid, correctly recorded, and approved at the time by this very system. The
contradiction was not committed; it was *inherited*, retroactively, the instant the worlds became one
continuity space.

There is nothing there to refuse. You cannot reject an event that already happened a thousand years
ago in a world that was following the rules. **The consolidation problem is the one shape of failure
a prevention layer cannot address**, which is why the White had to build something with the opposite
posture.

## What A Glitch Actually Is

This gives [[continuity-glitches]] a mechanism it has never had. There is a system whose stated job
is rejecting continuity-breaking events, and the player is looking at continuity-breaking events. One
of three things is happening, and the design has not yet decided which:

- **The contradiction was inherited, not committed** — the linking case, which this system has no
  purchase on at all, and which is handed to [[the-retcon-engine]].
- **The system is being bypassed** — a change made outside the process it inspects, which is exactly
  what the Red's and the Blue's unreviewed commits were.
- **The repair caused it.** The retcon engine patches locally and seeds contradictions out of scope,
  so a large share of what the player finds was *manufactured by the fix*, not merely uncovered by
  it. **This is the dominant source**, and the one that makes the count climb.

**"Continuity glitch" stops being a description and becomes the name of a specific failure.** The
term the design has used all along turns out to be diegetic.

## Ties

- [[quetzalcoatl-the-white]] — its author, and the first of his three stabilisation layers.
- [[tezcatlipoca-the-black]] — whose engine it exists to hold together.
- [[the-retcon-engine]] — the second layer, and what happens when this one does not fire.
- [[continuity-glitches]] — what the player sees when it does not hold.
- [[the-four-developers]] — the studio, and why the engine needed rescuing at all.
- [[faction-eleven-antagonist]] — the faction all of this belongs to.

## Player-Facing

**Not directly, and probably not by name for a very long time.** Its existence is only visible
through its absences: a world that mostly hangs together, and the specific places where it does not.

**Open:** whether the system is ever *encountered* — as a name in [[the-ancient-language]], as a
thing the liches can describe from outside ([[the-void]]), or never at all. A named mechanism the
player can learn about is a much louder reveal than an accumulated suspicion, and the game currently
runs on accumulated suspicion.
