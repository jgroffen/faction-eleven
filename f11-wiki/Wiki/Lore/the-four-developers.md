# The Four Developers

The four gods are **four software developers**, and they are **friends**. They built the games
themselves and put the company together themselves. Faction Eleven is not a world; it is a
**collection of ten computer games** they made. The realms are the games. The gods are the people
with commit access.

**Canon:** proposed · **Era:** outside the timeline

## The Story

Every divine act in [[the-four-gods]] is a change to a codebase:

| The god does                                          | The developer did                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The Black opens pathways between all realms           | argued that the stagnant games should be connected, and won — then connected them                                      |
| Realms descend into war and reality nears destruction | linking caused severe side effects across the games                                                                    |
| Three gods seal the gates with their combined power   | the White argued that closing the links would stabilise the games, and won — the three shut them down                   |
| Realms decay in isolation                             | unmaintained games rot — no patches, no new content                                                                    |
| The Red imbues [[ninja]] and [[val]]                  | a sneaked-in code change letting two characters unseal gates                                                           |
| The White learns of it and sets guardians             | a notification that characters are appearing in the wrong games, a look through the change history, and defensive code |
| The Blue seeds the [[the-guardians-of-night]]         | shipped a **Secret Society System**                                                                                    |
| Liches open gates into a void                         | AI-driven characters that hacked out onto the network ([[the-void]])                                                   |

**Time does not map.** A thousand years in a realm is an afternoon at a desk; the gods' clock and
the games' clock have no relationship, and the mismatch is a source of glitches rather than a
problem to be smoothed over ([[continuity-glitches]]).

## The Four

|           | Who                          | Seniority   | Age      |
| --------- | ---------------------------- | ----------- | -------- |
| **Black** | [[tezcatlipoca-the-black]]   | senior      | late 30s |
| **White** | [[quetzalcoatl-the-white]]   | senior      | late 40s |
| **Red**   | [[xipe-totec-the-red]]       | regular dev | late 20s |
| **Blue**  | [[huitzilopochtli-the-blue]] | regular dev | late 20s |

The Red is a woman; the other three are men.

**The two arguments in the studio are not the same argument.** The seniors are fighting about the
product — rebuild it or leave it alone. The two younger devs are fighting about **craft**, and they
sit at opposite ends of the quality-versus-velocity spectrum:

|          | The Red                                         | The Blue                                     |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Wants    | it right                                        | it done                                      |
| Builds   | big, elegant, complicated systems               | big, sweeping, untested changes              |
| Fails by | polishing the rock, scope creep, never shipping | not testing, not thinking about side-effects |
| Net      | ships almost nothing                            | **gets more done, at the cost of quality**   |

**Neither is a bad developer.** Each is missing exactly what the other has, and **they are best when
they work together** — which is the mechanism of the ending ([[story-outline-requirement]]). The
resolution is not one of the four being proved right; it is four people becoming a team that
functions.

## The Process Worked, Until It Didn't

The history divides cleanly in two, and the break is the whole plot.

**Agreed, argued, decided in the open:**

1. **The Black proposed linking the games**, because they had gone stagnant and connecting them would
   liven up the gameplay. He made the case and **won it** ([[the-linking-of-the-realms]]).
2. **The White proposed closing the links** when the games destabilised, as the way to stabilise them.
   He made the case and **won it** ([[the-long-disconnection]]).

Neither was seniority overriding anybody. Both were good-faith proposals that carried the room, and
between them they produced every large feature of the world the player finds.

**Then it stopped working.** Things held for a while, and then **both problems got worse at once** —
the instability was never cured, only contained, and the containment produced stagnation on top of it.
The four argued and argued and settled nothing.

3. **The Red sneaked a small change into the codebase** — the Red Power, granted to [[ninja]] and
   [[val]]. No proposal, no argument, no review. **This is the first illegitimate act in the entire
   history**, and it is the catalyst: it is what licenses
4. **the Blue's changes**, made the same way and much larger.

**Seniority explains why the break happened where it did.** The two who argued and won were the
seniors. The two who went around the process were the regular devs — a junior with no standing to win
an argument makes the change quietly instead. Every catastrophe in the present-day plot is an
unreviewed commit by someone who did not feel able to ask.

## Lead Developers

All four have worked on all ten games. **Lead** means who shaped a game, whose style is in it, and
who notices when it changes.

| Dev                          | Games                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| [[quetzalcoatl-the-white]]   | 01 [[institute-of-eight]] · 02 [[robots]]               |
| [[xipe-totec-the-red]]       | 03 [[celestials]] · 04 [[mystics]] · 05 [[fey-folk]]    |
| [[huitzilopochtli-the-blue]] | 06 [[pirates]] · 07 [[werebeasts]] · 09 [[green-skins]] |
| [[tezcatlipoca-the-black]]   | 08 [[the-damned]] · 10 [[aliens]]                       |

Three things fall out of this and none of them had to be invented:

- **The White got the notification** because 01 and 02 are his, and the guardian he added defends
  his own game.
- **The Black leads both 08 and 10**, which is why liches he built in one are looting the other, and
  why nobody else was watching that door ([[the-void]]).
- **The old war is the Red's arc against the Blue's arc.** The thousand-year conflict along the
  opposition axis of [[the-wheel-of-realms]] is two developers who disagree about improvements,
  fought by proxy.

**Shared access is load-bearing.** If a dev could only touch their own games, the player could
attribute any change by looking at which realm it happened in, and the mystery would collapse into
bookkeeping. All four having commit access to all ten is what makes attribution hard, blame
contestable, and the Red's reach into the Ninja's game possible at all.

Each dev has a **legible design signature** — a colour, a way of laying out space, a kind of enemy, a
tell in [[the-ancient-language]]. Learning to look at a piece of a realm and name whose hand made it
is a late-game verb, and the signatures read as craft as much as taste: the Red's realms are
elaborate, interlocking and unfinished; the Blue's are broad, fast and full of things that were never
checked.

## Avatars, And The Outside Leaking In

**Each of the four has an avatar, and it carries their colour.** The same avatar in three places:

1. **At work** — how they appear to each other in the studio.
2. **Inside the games** — the form they take when they are in a realm.
3. **In the leakage** — fragments of lore and of the real world that have got into the games, which
   the characters can find.

This is the **third evidence channel**, alongside [[the-ancient-language]] and
[[continuity-glitches]], and it is the most direct of the three. The language must be decoded and
the glitches must be inferred from; an avatar is simply *there*, in a realm, wearing a colour that
means something the finder has no way to interpret.

**It is also what makes the four legible as individuals rather than as a force.** A faction that has
forgotten the eleventh faction entirely can still notice that the same four colours keep recurring —
in a figure glimpsed at a gate, in a mark on a wall, in a fragment of text about a place with no name
— long before anyone can say what they are. Black, white, red and blue become a pattern the player
recognises before the game ever explains it.

## Why It Explains The Setting

Facts that were arbitrary become necessary:

- **Ten realms play as ten genres** ([[faction-genre-mechanics]]) because they are ten different
  games. [[realm-governs-game-style]] — the realm's mechanics apply to whoever stands in it — stops
  being a design rule and becomes a fact about which engine the code is running in.
- **Gates only reach near-by realms** ([[sealed-interplane-gates]]) because *near* means
  **compatible**. The wheel is a compatibility graph. Nobody chose to build gates only between
  neighbours; neighbours are the only places a gate can work.
- **Level one of navigation is the faction select screen** ([[nested-map-navigation]]) — a launcher.
- **Ten factions have ten different fictional excuses for respawning** ([[death-and-return]]) and ten
  separate currencies ([[faction-currencies]]) because they are ten checkpoint systems and ten
  economies.

## The Studio

**Four friends who met through work or study and built this themselves.** No publisher, no owner, no
investor, no deadline — there is nothing above them because they made the thing above them. The ladder
does not merely *stop* at the four; there is no rung to stop at.

**Their differences were never the problem.** Four people who work in four different ways built ten
games together and it worked. What changed is not them; it is the **stakes**:

| | The stake |
|---|---|
| 1 | The games they care about are becoming **unstable** |
| 2 | The company they built could **go under** |
| 3 | The friend group could **break up** |

Each one follows from the last. The company failing is not an outside business problem — it is what
happens if they stop being able to work together, which is what happens if the friendship goes. **All
three stakes are the same stake, seen at three distances.**

**Pressure is what turned difference into conflict.** They are still friends. Nothing has broken yet.
That is the tense the whole thing is in, and it is why there is still something for the protagonists
to save.

**The disagreement is technical, not strategic.** The four cannot agree how to fix a system that is
failing:

- **[[tezcatlipoca-the-black]]** says it is beyond repair and should be rebuilt.
- **[[quetzalcoatl-the-white]]** says it should be protected and left alone.
- **[[xipe-totec-the-red]]** says it should be done properly, and has not finished designing how.
- **[[huitzilopochtli-the-blue]]** says it should be done now, and keeps shipping fixes nobody asked
  for.

Every one of them is trying to save it. **The arguing is what may break them up.**

There are **no real-world business problems in this story.** The four are a group of friends dealing
with a shared problem they cannot agree on, and that is the whole of the outside world.

## The Solution Is Their Differences

**Compromise and collaboration.** Not one approach winning, but the four leveraging their differences
to balance each other:

- The **Red's** insistence on getting it right is the brake the **Blue** has never had.
- The **Blue's** willingness to ship is the push the **Red** has never had.
- The **Black's** readiness to tear something down is what the **White** needs in order to stop
  defending a thing that is failing.
- The **White's** care for what already exists is what stops the **Black** throwing away what still
  works.

**Each of them is the correction to somebody else.** That is why no one of them is right and no one
of them is wrong, and it is why the answer was never going to be a winner.

**It is all or nothing.** They succeed together, or they lose the company and the friendships both.

**There is no diegetic player**, either. An authority above the argument would make the argument
unanswerable and beside the point. Four friends falling out over something they love is more
unsettling than any cosmic observer, because there is nobody who can overrule it.

## The Instability

The games are not merely rotting from neglect. **They are actively destabilising**, and the fault is
cumulative — the unrepaired damage from [[the-linking-of-the-realms]], a thousand years of no
maintenance, three sets of unreviewed changes made by people working around each other, and
something loose in the network that none of them knows about ([[the-void]]).

**This raises the Black's argument from preference to diagnosis.** He does not want to rebuild because
he is bored of it; he wants to rebuild because it is getting worse and nothing anyone has tried has
held.

**Design consequence:** the rate of [[continuity-glitches]] should **climb across the chapters**,
because the world really is coming apart. The player's growing sense that something is wrong is not
mood — it is measurement.

## What Fixes It

The protagonists. Everything the player does to move the story is something that helps the four sort
their problems out ([[story-outline-requirement]]). The ending is not one developer winning the
argument; it is four friends finding a way to work together again, and the game they made surviving
because of it.

**The two layers of this story say the same thing.** In the fiction, ten factions must unite and hold
a lasting balance, and the conclusion the protagonists reach is that no single god is wholly right or
wholly wrong. Underneath, four friends must compromise and use what each of them is good at.

**The protagonists' solution is the developers' solution.** Not an echo of it, not a metaphor for it —
the same move, made twice, at two scales. That is what makes it possible for the in-world goal and
the goal beneath it to converge in a single ending rather than merely rhyme.

## Are They Anyone?

The developers believe none of the characters are sentient. **The liches provably are** — they were
built as AI, and they broke containment ([[the-void]]). Everyone else is unresolved on purpose,
which makes the White's position a bet rather than a fact: he argues for the survival of things he
cannot prove are anyone. The one faction demonstrably awake is [[the-damned]] — hostile,
gate-breaching, corpse-stealing, and the game's least sympathetic faction.

## Player-Facing

**The endgame reveal, delivered in artifacts and never in a scene.** No playable sequence at the
studio: a dev scene turns the best mystery in the design into an ordinary twist and demands four
human characters the game has no other use for. The four stay **presences with hands** — a change
appears, a boss exists that did not yesterday, a notification fires somewhere out of frame.

Four channels carry it: decoded [[the-ancient-language]]; accumulated [[continuity-glitches]]; the
**avatars and the leaked fragments of the outside**, which are the most direct and the earliest
available; and the liches, who are the only entities that have been outside and can testify.
