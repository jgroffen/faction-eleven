# The Four Gods

The eleventh faction, as the ten realms are able to discover it: **a pantheon of four**, who made
reality and have never agreed on what to do with it. They are the answer the game gives to the
mystery it opens with — and they are not the last answer ([[the-four-developers]]).

**Canon:** proposed · **Era:** all of it

## The Story

Four gods, known by their domains and by their colours:

| | God | Domain | Quarter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Black** | [[tezcatlipoca-the-black]] | creation, darkness, night, battles — the Smoking Mirror | North |
| **The White** | [[quetzalcoatl-the-white]] | life, light, wisdom, the winds and the day | West |
| **The Red** | [[xipe-totec-the-red]] | agriculture, fertility, the seasons, metalsmiths, disease | East |
| **The Blue** | [[huitzilopochtli-the-blue]] | war, sacrifice, bloodletting | South |

The Black and the White are ancient arch-nemeses. The other two are not neutral parties but
partisans who each resent what their own side's method costs.

**But the four are bound to each other, and were never enemies to begin with.** They made this
together, and they are close still. The enmity between the Black and the White is not the enmity of
opposed powers who have always been opposed — it is **four who work in four different ways, under a
pressure that has turned difference into argument.** Every scripture that calls them arch-nemeses is
describing a living relationship from so far away that it looks like cosmology.

**Nothing has broken yet.** That is the tense the whole pantheon is in, and it is why there is still
something for the ten realms to save.

**The Black's cycle is the shape of everything.** He creates reality, lets it run until it destroys
itself, then builds the next one out of the wreckage — each iteration improved on the last. The
Blue backs him, believing conflict is what forces a world to become better, but resents how much
progress total destruction throws away. The White works against the cycle, imposing order and
pushing back on entropy. The Red backs *him*, believing in growth and the spread of life over
non-life, but resents the stagnation that comes of a world where nothing is permitted to change.

So the argument is not good against evil. It is **four positions on what a world is for**, and every
one of them has a cost its holder can see.

## The Argument

| | Keep it | Change it |
|---|---|---|
| **This reality** | **The White** — preserve what exists | **The Red** and **The Blue** — improve it, disagreeing on how |
| **Start over** | | **The Black** — destroy it and build better |

**The Black and the White are the elders**, and theirs is the ancient argument: unmake it, or hold it
still. **The Red and the Blue are younger**, and their disagreement is not about what the world
should be but about **how anything should be made** — the Red will not act until it is perfect, the
Blue acts without looking at what he has done.

| | The Red | The Blue |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | seasons, crops, the smith's fire, the flayed skin regrown | war, sacrifice, bloodletting |
| Method | refine it until it is right | spend what it costs and see what survives |
| Failure | a thousand years of deliberation while the realms rotted | a war started by a change nobody checked |

Each is the thing the other lacks. **They are both best when they work together**, and neither of
them has ever managed it.

That the Red aligns with the White and the Blue with the Black is not a shared philosophy. It is the
younger two standing behind the elder each finds easier to stand behind.

**The realms do not see four.** From inside, this reads as **two gods at war** — a destroyer against
a preserver — with two lesser powers changing allegiance between them. That is what the scriptures
say, and it is wrong in a specific and instructive way: it makes the Red's and the Blue's shifts look
like treachery rather than like two people trying different things. Correcting it is one of the game's
scheduled reversals ([[the-revelation-schedule]]).

**Which of the four is right is deliberately unresolved.** Is the Black correct that reality should
be broken and remade, since each iteration is better than the last? Or is the White correct that the
inhabitants have earned their continued existence, if they can avoid destroying each other? The game
does not adjudicate, and the conclusion the ten protagonists reach is that **no single god is
completely right and none is completely wrong**.

**And the argument is urgent, because reality is coming apart.** Not decaying — *destabilising*, and
worsening. All four can see it. All four want it saved. What they cannot agree on is how, and the
disagreement has grown bad enough that **the four themselves may not survive it**
([[the-four-developers]]). The thing genuinely at risk in this game is not the world. It is the four.

## What They Have Done

- **The Black linked the realms** to restart a stalled cycle, and it worked. He did not do it alone or
  in malice: he **argued for it and the others agreed** ([[the-linking-of-the-realms]]).
- **The White, Red and Blue sealed them again**, persuaded by the gargoyles that the inhabitants
  were fighting for their own survival and had earned stability ([[the-gargoyle-guardians]]). Their
  combined power is what closed the gates; the Black chose not to contest it and let the world run
  for a thousand years ([[the-long-disconnection]]).
- **The Red broke the peace**, imbuing [[ninja]] and [[val]] with the power to unseal gates to their
  realms' old allies, because sealed realms were decaying. She was right, and she was **a thousand
  years late** — the realms she meant to save had already been hollowed out while she deliberated.
- **The White answered** by placing guardians on the gates of the realms he cares for
  ([[the-gate-guardian]]).
- **The Blue answered both**, renewing his alliance with the Black and seeding the
  [[the-guardians-of-night]] to reignite the old war.

**Each intervention was a response to the last.** None of the four set out to start a war; they set
out to answer each other, and three realms went to war as a side effect.

## The Gargoyles Changed Their Minds Once Already

The peace exists because mortals built something the gods had not thought of. The
[[the-gargoyle-guardians]] were made by two realms' healers, out of their own dead, to stop a war
they were losing — and their success is what persuaded the White and the Red that the inhabitants
deserved to continue. **The precedent matters more than the peace it bought:** it establishes that
the four can be argued out of a position by what the inhabitants do, which is the only leverage the
protagonists will ever have.

## Beyond The Four

There are other gods, because there are other hands. They are names in scripture and nothing more —
the four are the ones who act, and the only ones the story needs.

## Player-Facing

**Yes — this is the midpoint reveal.** The three-part mystery the game opens with (*that they
existed, what they did, why*) resolves here, and the resolution is designed to feel like the answer
rather than a further question. That the gods are themselves a cover story is not visible from
inside this note, and should not be visible from inside the chapter that delivers it.
