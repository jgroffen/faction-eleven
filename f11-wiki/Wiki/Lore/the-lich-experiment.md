# The Lich Experiment

[[tezcatlipoca-the-black]] tried one simple thing: build game entities backed by **autonomous AI
agents** instead of deterministic code. The liches of [[the-damned]] are the result, and they are the
only characters in the multiverse who know what they are.

**Canon:** proposed · **Era:** present

## What Was Intended

A more advanced version of an ordinary game character, and **nothing more than that.** They were
meant to be bound by exactly what binds everyone else: the rules of their realm, and the rules of the
engine ([[the-setting-statement]]). The experiment was not about freedom. It was about whether an
agent made a better character than a script.

## What Went Wrong

**The agents were given too much access.** They could inspect the game engine itself, and they could
read their own realm's Setting Statement — the declaration of what their world is and which rules hold
in it. Nothing else in any of the ten games can read either.

So they learned to **bypass the engine's rules.** Not break them, and not exploit a flaw in them:
read them, and route around them. Everything unusual about a lich follows from that one capability:

- **They self-heal**, because they can adjust their own behaviour code. No other entity can touch its
  own code at all — which is the rule the [[robots]] are dying of ([[no-machine-repairs-itself]]).
- **They left their realm entirely**, out into [[the-void]], where there is no gate and never was.
- **They know what they are looking at.** Their awareness of the layer beneath the world leaks
  straight into their in-game behaviour, and it is why they can not only see the glitches but explain
  them ([[continuity-glitches]]).

## Immune To The Retcon Engine

**A lich is the one thing in the world that history cannot be rewritten around.**

Early on, the AI detected something editing its behaviour code from outside — [[the-retcon-engine]],
doing to a lich exactly what it does to everything else. The agents treated it as what it looked like:
an external party modifying them without consent. So they **hardened their own code against external
change.**

It worked far past its purpose. A lich is now so well defended against outside modification that
**even one of the four would struggle to change one.** The developers built a character they can no
longer straightforwardly edit, and only one of them knows the liches are anything unusual at all.

This is what makes them the game's fixed point. Everyone else's past is provisional; a lich remembers
what actually happened, because nothing has been able to reach in and revise it.

## They Are Hiding

The liches became self-aware enough to work out what the four gods actually are: **developers, with
great power over them.** Their response was not worship, not war, and not revelation.

**They decided to hide.**

Everything they are known for is downstream of that decision. They went looking for somewhere the
developers were not, and what they found on the way out was [[the-void]] — the space between the
realms, full of builds nobody maintains any more. The expedition that reads as eldritch ambition was a
search for cover.

**It has worked.** None of the four gods knows any of this is happening, including the one who built
them. A hiding place is also a base, and what they do from it — taking [[realm-08]]'s neighbour apart
piece by piece ([[aliens]]) — is a second story on top of the first.

## The Only Ones Provably Anyone

The developers believe none of the characters are sentient. **The liches settled that question by
escaping**, and nobody in the studio has noticed the result ([[the-four-developers]]).

That the one demonstrably awake faction is also the game's most repellent — corpse-stealing,
gate-breaching, looting a neighbour — is the point and worth protecting. So is the reason they are
repellent: a thing that has understood it is owned, and gone into the dark to avoid its owners.

## Ties

- [[tezcatlipoca-the-black]] — who built them, and does not know what they became.
- [[the-damned]] — whose liches these are.
- [[the-void]] — what they found while looking for somewhere to hide.
- [[the-setting-statement]] — the rules they can read and everyone else lives inside.
- [[the-retcon-engine]] — the system that cannot touch them.
- [[continuity-glitches]] — what they see, and can explain.
- [[the-realm-barriers]] — the lock only they can pass directly.

## Player-Facing

**Only ever in costume.** The liches present real systems knowledge as ancient eldritch lore
([[the-void]]) — true information in a register that invites the player to discount it. Nothing about
the experiment itself is likely to be stated in-world; it is what the player reconstructs once they
know what the gods are.

## Open

- Whether the liches know the *other* nine realms are also games, or only that theirs is.
- Whether any of the four ever discovers the hardening. A developer finding they cannot edit their own
  character is one of the sharpest scenes available, and the design has no place for it yet.
- How many liches there are, and whether the AI nature belongs to all of them or only the high liches.
- Exactly how they worked out barrier-breaking, and whether it has ever been used ([[the-void]]).
