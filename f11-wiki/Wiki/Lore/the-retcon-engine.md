# The Retcon Engine

When a god changes a realm, **the realm writes history to explain the change**. A boss appears at a
gate, and within a generation there is a family sentenced to fight it, a treason they committed, and
a shrine where their punishment is recorded. None of it happened. All of it is true now.

**Canon:** proposed · **Era:** whenever a god intervenes

## The Story

The games absorb what is done to them. A code change arrives with no history attached, and the game
generates one — plausible, detailed, and load-bearing for everyone who lives inside it.

**The generated lore is internally consistent and externally contradictory.** That rule is the whole
design:

- Inside one realm, the story is airtight. The [[institute-of-eight]] can explain its own generational
  punishment perfectly, in detail, with documents.
- Across two realms, it falls apart. The Institute cannot explain why the [[robots]] — a
  historically friendly neighbour — needed guarding at all.

So the player never catches a lie by listening harder. They catch it by **carrying a fact from one
realm into another**, which is precisely what a game with ten protagonists is built to do. Seamless
retconning would give the player nothing to find; the seams are the evidence
([[continuity-glitches]]).

## The Worked Example

[[the-gate-guardian]] is the clearest case and the one the player stands inside from chapter one.
[[quetzalcoatl-the-white]] added protective code to the gates of the realms he leads. The
[[institute-of-eight]] now has a boss nobody placed, guarding a door nobody closed — and a complete
account of why: [[ninja]]'s family are traitors, sentenced to fight it endlessly, generation after
generation.

The sentence is a fiction the realm wrote to make sense of a change from outside. **Nobody in the
Institute is lying.** The records are real, the shame is real, and generations of a family have
lived and died inside an explanation that was generated to cover an edit.

## What It Does Not Smooth Over

The engine is not perfect, and it works hardest on things it cannot fully absorb:

- **Timescale.** The gods' clock has no relationship to the realms' ([[the-four-developers]]). A
  guardian placed recently must be given a history older than anyone's memory, and the arithmetic
  does not always close.
- **Objects from elsewhere.** Things that arrive from another game have no local history to be given
  one.
- **Contradiction with a surviving witness.** [[gargoyle]] holds an unedited memory of the world
  before the torpor, which makes him the one character able to notice that the past has moved.

## Player-Facing

**Indirectly and constantly.** The player is never told that lore self-heals; they are given a realm
whose explanations are excellent and whose explanations do not agree with the realm next door. The
realisation that *history here is written after the fact, by something* is one of the largest turns
in the game, and it arrives by accumulation rather than announcement.
