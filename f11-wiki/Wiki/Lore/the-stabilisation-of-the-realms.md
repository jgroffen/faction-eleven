# The Stabilisation Of The Realms

How the game ends. **Rough** — the shape is settled and the detail is not.

**Canon:** proposed · **Era:** the end of the game

## The Story

**The protagonists of all ten realms work together to stabilise the realms and eliminate the
glitches** ([[continuity-glitches]]) — and they do it **while the links between realms are reopening**,
not by closing them again.

That is the whole reversal. The realms were linked and it caused catastrophe; they were sealed and
they rotted ([[the-linking-of-the-realms]], [[the-long-disconnection]]). Both of the senior
developers' answers were *whole-system* answers, argued and won on their merits, and both failed. What
the protagonists demonstrate is the third option nobody proposed: **connected and stable at the same
time**, achieved from inside, by the inhabitants.

## What It Shows The Developers

**That there is a better way, and that it is each other.** The four can **work together and lean on
each other's strengths** to fix their games — **instead of restarting from scratch**
([[the-four-developers]]).

The gods' war has always been the devs arguing ([[the-revelation-schedule]]). The ending is the
argument being settled, and it is settled not by one of them winning but by ten characters
demonstrating that the strengths the four keep using against each other combine.

**And a second thread, which is the game's quietest and best joke: to the developers the games appear
to have *self-stabilised*.** They do not see ten protagonists forming an alliance. They see metrics
coming good on their own — and conclude that these games deserve to be **allowed to exist and be
supported**.

So the ten realms save themselves by being mistaken for having recovered.

## Why It Works

- **It makes the premise pay out.** Ten stories that intertwine ([[faction-eleven-premise]]) end in the
  only thing ten stories can do that one cannot.
- **It is reachable from the revelation ladders.** The conclusion — *no single god is completely right
  and none is completely wrong* — is [[the-revelation-schedule]]'s stated destination, and this is what
  the characters do with it.
- **It never breaks the no-outside-view rule.** The protagonists do not address the developers, and the
  developers never see the protagonists. Each side reads the other's actions as their own kind of event.

## No Realm Has To Be Finished

**Completing an individual realm's own game is not required for Faction Eleven to be complete.** The
[[mystics]]' end-boss, and every other realm's, is content a player may reach or not
([[mystics-isometric-action-rpg]]). The main story runs through the alliance, not through ten
separate victories — which is what keeps a ten-genre game shippable at all
([[project-scope-and-constraints]]).

## Player-Facing

Yes — it is the ending. How the "self-stabilised" reading is delivered without a cutaway to the studio
is the hard problem, since nothing in the game may stand outside the realms
([[the-revelation-schedule]]).

## Open

- **Almost all of it.** The shape above is the author's own rough sketch.
- What "stabilising a realm" is as an *action* — what the protagonists actually do, and whether it is a
  final game mode ([[faction-unique-features]]).
- How the player is shown the developers' reading without an outside view.
- Whether all ten protagonists survive it, and whether any realm refuses.
- What happens to the [[faction-eleven-antagonist|eleventh faction]] and to the
  [[the-secret-society-system]] that is still running unwatched.
- Whether the liches who left the world ([[the-void]]) come back for this.
