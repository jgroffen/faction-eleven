# The Convergence At The Monastery

The culmination of a chapter, and the first time protagonists from four factions stand in the same
place: **[[ninja-kazuma]], [[mifix]] and [[gargoyle-granite]] reach [[templar-ermengarda]] and [[conjurer-voisin]]**, who are
already together. Then the five of them decide what to do.

**Status:** design · **Type:** main · **Chapter:** [[chapter-03]] · **Location:**
[[templar-monastery]]

## The Beat

Two arrivals, in order. The Conjurer is first, in answer to [[the-wand]]
([[the-answering-of-the-wand]]). Then the Ninja, Mifix and the Gargoyle come following the faint
signal they have been chasing since [[the-second-signal]].

They arrive at a monastery under siege by a war machine the Celestials cannot beat, holding a relic
the Celestials do not understand — and a Templar and Conjurer who have just worked out what it is.

## What It Unlocks

- **[[mifix]] meets [[odie]].** The corrupted memory meets the intact one, and the lost history of
  the Robot Realm can finally be told ([[no-machine-repairs-itself]] is why only one of them still
  has it).
- **Hints toward `ACCESS DENIED`.** Odie knows something about what is needed to unblock the
  autonomous-robotics branch of [[autofix-skill-tree]] — the [[robots]]' forbidden future.
- **The Templar gets his ancient allies**, which is what he retreated to the monastery to find.
- **[[odie]] names what the Ninja is** — a carrier of [[the-red-power]], able to breach a sealed
  gate.

## The Council

What each party brings:

- **The Ninja, Mifix and the Gargoyle say plainly what they want: to reach the [[robots]].**
- **The Templar and Conjurer have worked out what the relics are** — links to ancient allies and a
  way to call for help ([[allied-faction-signals]]) — and that **Mifix is the same thing for the
  [[institute-of-eight]]**: the Institute's own link to an old ally, kept as an ancestor spirit.
- **Odie does not know why he is in the Celestial realm.** He was switched off before whatever
  put him there was explained to him, if it ever was.

What none of them has: **the way to the Robots.** Odie knows the interplane gates exist but not
where any are. The Celestials' awareness of their own gates has faded — they hold two open ones by
necessity and could not find a sealed one. The Gargoyle knows the network's *shape*
([[realm-nearness-and-traversal]]) — so everyone present can agree a Celestials↔Robots gate exists —
but not where in [[realm-03]] it physically sits.

**The decision: seek out a [[werebeasts|Werebeast]] gargoyle.** Four reasons, one per party:

1. **An intact gargoyle knows where the gates are** — gargoyles carry the whole network as fact, and
   a functioning one can point to the door nobody in the room can find.
2. **The Gargoyle can find out whether he is malfunctioning.** He does not trust [[val]]'s verdict
   and a machine could not read him ([[the-diagnosis-of-the-last-gargoyle]]); another gargoyle is the
   one judge he would trust on whether trying to stop the war is what he was built for, even though
   it looks like acting against the [[green-skins]]' wishes.
3. **If the Werebeast gargoyles can be reactivated, they should seal the gate to the [[mystics]].**
   Which gate, and why the Werebeast gargoyles need reactivating at all, is open — see
   [[the-mystics-second-secret-society]].
4. **Val will try to destroy them if she can reach them.** She has resolved to destroy every
   remaining gargoyle ([[the-trial-of-the-last-gargoyle]]); the intact set in [[realm-09]] is her
   next target, and they need warning.

## Why It Matters

It is the first time the game's structure delivers on its premise in full: four protagonists,
four genres, four realms, one room. Everything before it is signals; this is arrival.

It also resolves the Celestials' framing. The Templar believed the Celestials needed **ancient
allies** to defeat the war machine and chose where to retreat on that belief. He turns out to be
right — and the allies were partly in his own cellar the whole time.

And it turns the game's premise into something the characters themselves have said: the devices in
the shrines are how allies once called each other, and the way home for two of the parties in the
room runs through a door that only a guardian remembers.
