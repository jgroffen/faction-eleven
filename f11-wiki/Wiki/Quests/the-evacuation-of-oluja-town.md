# The Evacuation Of Oluja Town

The [[celestials]]' second defeat, and **the first one they choose.** Word reaches
[[templar-monastery]] that the green-skin war machine is heading for [[oluja-town]];
[[templar-will]] goes, joins [[knight-ermengarda]] there, and the two of them hold waves of
attackers **while the town empties behind them**. When the machine itself arrives they abandon the
position and fall back to the monastery, the Knight leading the townsfolk out.

**Status:** design · **Type:** main · **Chapter:** [[chapter-02]] · **Location:** [[oluja-town]]

## Hook

The signals have gone out and nothing has answered yet ([[the-calling-of-ancient-allies]]). Then a
rider, and a place on the map that is not a fortification — a town, with people in it, in the path
of the thing that took [[the-outpost]].

## Objectives

1. **Reach the town.** The Templar leaves the monastery on the news.
2. **Hold the waves.** Green-skin attackers, fought as [[celestials-hero-tower-defence]] — with
   **two hero units on the field**, the Templar and the Knight, for the first time.
3. **Get the town out.** The objective is not the position. The defence is scored by what leaves
   behind it.
4. **Abandon it when the machine arrives.** The war machine is not a wave; it is the end of the
   battle.
5. **Fall back to [[templar-monastery]]**, with the Knight leading the townsfolk.

## The Beat

**A defence whose win condition is elsewhere.** The player is not holding ground and is not meant
to; they are buying minutes for people crossing behind the line. It is the same tower defence the
faction has played twice, with the meaning of "holding" changed — and it gives
[[celestials-hero-tower-defence]] its clearest answer to the problem that this faction keeps losing:
here, losing the ground *is* the win.

**And it is the first time the Templar has company.** [[knight-ermengarda]] fights beside him and
talks to him — the Celestials' decisions stop being a commander's internal monologue at exactly
the point the faction is asked to give something up.

## Why It Matters

- It **names and spends a place**: [[oluja-town]] exists, is defended, and is lost, all in one
  chapter — which is what gives the Knight something to go back for.
- It **pays off the retreat pattern** without repeating it. The outpost was a rout the player could
  not prevent ([[the-fall-of-the-outpost]]); this one is a commander spending a position on
  purpose.
- It **closes the Celestials' chapter two** on a defeat that does not feel like one, while the
  chapter's other three threads end on cliffhangers ([[chapter-02]]).

## Prerequisites

Follows [[the-calling-of-ancient-allies]] — the Templar is at the monastery, and the signals are
already away, when the news comes.

## Open

- **Who brings the word**, and how far the town is from the monastery.
- Whether the evacuation is a visible mechanic — civilians crossing the field, a counter — or a
  timer the player is told about.
- Whether the player controls the Knight, or she fights under her own logic beside the Templar
  ([[knight-ermengarda]]).
- What losing "properly" means here: whether a player who holds too long loses townsfolk, and
  whether the game tracks how many got out.
- Whether the war machine's arrival is playable at all, or a scripted end to the battle.
